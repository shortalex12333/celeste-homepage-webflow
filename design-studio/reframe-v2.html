What CelesteOS does in the background

You don’t know what you don’t know.

Five workers running while the boat runs. Each one is a draft, not an action. Each result is cited. Nothing leaves without your sign-off.

Worker 01Warranty

Money about to be spent.

It is Tuesday morning. A fault is logged against the port-side generator. The bridge does not know it. The yard does not know it. The supplier’s warranty desk does not know it.

CelesteOS does. The moment the fault is raised, the warranty worker matches the equipment to its purchase record, its installation date, and its manufacturer’s clause. A claim pack assembles itself in the background — fault history, photos, work order, the cited warranty paragraph — addressed to the supplier’s warranty desk and ready to forward.

You read it. You forward. The supplier pays.

Worker 03Receiving

Discrepancies on the dock.

A truck arrives at the yard. Twelve cartons. The PO has fifteen lines. Nobody is checking line-by-line against the packing list at 16:40 on a Friday. The signature happens. The shortage is discovered six weeks later at the quarterly count, and by then the supplier’s claim window has closed.

The receiving worker takes a photo of the packing list, matches it line-by-line to the PO, and surfaces the delta — the three items not delivered, the two delivered in the wrong quantity, the one delivered in the wrong part number. A drafted discrepancy email comes back, ready to forward to the supplier before the carton seal is broken.

The shipment goes back before it is signed for.

Worker 04Repeat-fault

Faults already diagnosed.

The fault is new to the engineer logging it. It is not new to the boat. The same fault, on the same equipment, has been diagnosed and fixed twice before — once by an engineer who has rotated off, once by an engineer two engineers before that. Both diagnoses are in the record. Neither is on the new engineer’s desk.

The repeat-fault worker watches every new fault as it is logged, scans the full operational history for the same equipment, and surfaces the prior diagnoses the moment the match is made. Before the marine technician’s callout is booked. Before the wrong part is ordered for the third time. Before the consultancy hour is authorised.

The boat does not pay to discover what the boat already knows.

Worker 05Cross-domain

Answers across email, manuals, and the record.

The question is small. The answer is in three places that do not talk to each other. A manual sentence on page 187. An email reply from the manufacturer’s technical desk last August. A work-order note from a chief engineer who has since rotated off. Today’s engineer does not know any of them are connected.

The cross-domain worker treats the boat’s manuals, faults, parts, work orders, certificates, and email as one record. One question, every domain. The answer comes back with every source cited — manual page, work order ID, email thread — visible beside the answer.

No hallucinations. If CelesteOS cannot find a source, it says so.
