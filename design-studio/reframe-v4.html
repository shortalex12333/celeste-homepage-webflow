(02)Workers

You don’t know what you don’t know.

Five workers running in the background — pointed at the moments where money quietly leaves the boat, or where a question goes unanswered because nobody knew to ask it. Each one is a draft, not an action. You read, you decide, you sign.

Worker 01Warranty

Money about to be spent.

The moment a fault is raised against a piece of equipment still under manufacturer warranty, the warranty worker surfaces it — with a forward-ready claim pack: fault history, photos, work order, the cited warranty clause. You forward. The supplier pays. The €18,000 you were about to authorise does not leave the operating budget.

Worker 02Survey

Certificates before they expire.

Sixty days before a class survey, the survey worker gathers every certificate the surveyor will ask for — and flags the three that expire inside the window — into one signed bundle. The week-before-survey scramble does not happen. The new chief engineer is not the one explaining to the captain why two certificates lapsed during the previous rotation.

Worker 03Receiving

Discrepancies on the dock.

A photo of the packing list goes in. The receiving worker matches PO lines, calculates the inventory delta, and drafts the discrepancy email to the supplier. The shipment goes back before it is signed for. The €4,000 of parts you would have paid for but never received does not enter the books as “mystery shortage at the next quarterly count.”

Worker 04Repeat-fault

Faults already diagnosed.

The same fault, on the same equipment, three rotations ago. The diagnosis is in the record. The repeat-fault worker surfaces it the moment the new fault is logged — before the marine technician’s callout is booked, before the €2,400 of consultancy is authorised, before the same impeller is ordered for the third time.

Worker 05Cross-domain

Answers across email and manuals.

“Which generator setting did the manufacturer recommend after last summer’s overheating?” The cross-domain worker answers, with the email thread and the manual page beside the answer. Three places that have always been kept separately, queried as one record, every claim cited.

Every worker is a draft. CelesteOS proposes; the crew decides. Nothing auto-files, nothing auto-sends, nothing auto-asserts.

(04)The boat’s truth

The vessel that knows its own history is the vessel that keeps its value.

Every fault. Every diagnosis. Every signed handover. The boat’s own ground truth — not the operator’s word for it.

Every action CelesteOS records is timestamped, attributed, and sealed once signed. Not as a legal device. As the simple property of a record that is true: if it was written, it stays written, in the order it happened.

Anyone can verify any record at any time, without a CelesteOS account and without our involvement. The boat owns the proof of the boat’s history. CelesteOS holds the key for the boat, not against it.
