Design studio · Reframe prototypes

Five takes on the §11 site reframe.

Each prototype is a full homepage, faithful to the §11 plan: handover-led, no receipts surface, no Integrity Score, no insurance, continuity guarantee included. They differ only in visual rhythm, structural ordering, and which buyer they speak to first.

V1 · Surgical
Plan-literal edit.
Closest to the current production site.

Keeps the current dark hero with the search-artefact panel. Reorders services 01→02→03 (handover / search / consolidate). Drops the "Proof, when proof matters" service. Adds the new "What CelesteOS searches for you" catalog. Trust posture kept; Trust removed from primary nav. Pricing $450 unchanged.

Buyer: engineer-first Vibe: production-tier Risk: low
V2 · Question-anchor
Editorial single-column.
Light mode, Cormorant Garamond italics, B&O / Rolex restraint.

Each section is a question with an answer. "A chief engineer leaves on a Tuesday." → "By Friday morning, the boat already knows everything." Light mode, 720px column, deliberate whitespace. Five missions rendered one per scroll. Buyer band addresses the office. Lowest density, highest editorial weight.

Buyer: equal voice Vibe: editorial / magazine Risk: medium (slow scan)
V3 · Mission catalog
Linear / Stripe-style tile grid.
Sticky nav, hero stat-tiles, mission cards in a 3-up grid.

Hero: "One question. Every record on the boat." Big painkiller card (text + doc visual). Five mission cards in a 3-up grid with a sixth "anything else you teach it" placeholder. Two-column Continuity + Trust reassurance. Big pricing card. Sticky nav with a contrasting pilot CTA — the most browse-able / scan-friendly variant.

Buyer: scanner-first Vibe: modern SaaS Risk: low
V4 · Two-persona split
Engineer | Office, side-by-side.
Dark left half / light right half. Both audiences read first.

Literal split hero — left pane (dark) speaks to the chief engineer; right pane (light) speaks to the management company. Below, a unified band: the five missions in a strip both audiences need. Three reassurance cards: Continuity / Trust / Founder bio. Names the buyer explicitly without burying the user.

Buyer: explicit split Vibe: editorial / asymmetric Risk: medium (visual commitment)
V5 · Anti-pitch
"What CelesteOS is not, and what it is."
Inversion of the SaaS norm — leads with what's been removed.

Hero is three crossed-out sentences with the truthful replacement underneath ("CelesteOS replaces your PMS sits alongside it. CelesteOS predicts your insurance premium writes your handover. CelesteOS scores your boat answers one question at a time.") Four "NOT / IS" rows directly address Flaws #2, #4, #5 from the pressure test. Five missions as a numbered list. "The boring honest part" block answers buyer-questions upfront. Riskiest tonally — but most-defensible against any "you're hiding something" read.

Buyer: defensive / objection-led Vibe: contrarian editorial Risk: medium-high (tonal commitment)

The §11-L1 test, against every variant

  1. A captain reading this homepage for 90 seconds walks away able to say one sentence: "They write your handover for you, search every record on the boat, and save you money on what's already under warranty — and they never claim anything they can't cite."
  2. The painkiller (handover) is leading.
  3. The vitamin (search) is supporting.
  4. The liability (receipts) is invisible on the sales surface.
  5. The §4 coverage rule (drafts + citations + crew confirms) is felt without being lectured.
  6. The word insurance, the phrase Integrity Score, and liability is the… framing never appear.
  7. Continuity & portability are stated — the solo-founder bus-factor objection (Flaw #4) is answered before it's asked.
