Philosophy

(01)
Most systems expect crews to know where things live. That fails under pressure, fatigue, and rotation. CelesteOS removes navigation entirely. Every manual, work order, fault record, and email, searchable as a single operational record. The vessel's knowledge stays in the vessel.

Built by an ETO who lived this problem. Alex Short spent five years as an Electro-Technical Officer, then Head of Department, on superyachts up to 122 metres, including Maryah, Legend, Freedom, Nero, and Whisper. He inherited vessels mid-rotation and watched operational knowledge walk off the gangway with every handover.

CelesteOS is the system he wished existed at that moment.

The record (05)
The record that answers the surveyor.
When the question comes — who did this, when, on whose authority — the answer is already on the page. Every read, every write, every signature is timestamped against the vessel and attributable to the person. The record is append-only: it only ever grows. Nothing is edited away after the fact.
Every action, on the record
Who, what, when — already written down.
Each entity carries its own trail. A status change, a note, a linked part — each one stamped with the person who did it and the moment it happened. Nothing is reconstructed from memory at the survey. The trail was written as the work was done.
Append-only
It only ever grows.
The ledger is one running history of the vessel — documents read, work orders moved, handovers signed off — in the order it happened. Entries are added, never overwritten. The record you show a surveyor next year is the same record that was written today.
Attributable
Signed by the person who did the work.
A handover closes with a signature against the engineer who held the work and the head of department who reviewed it. Authority is on the page, not assumed. When someone asks on whose say-so a decision was made, the name is already there.
