# Celeste Product Imagery Doctrine

## What This Is

This is the governing creative doctrine for all CelesteOS product imagery. Every worker involved in image production must read this before creating anything. No exceptions.

Celeste imagery is not UI documentation. It is cinematic interface storytelling.

---

## The Strategic Goal

Every image must do at least 2 of these:

1. Stop the scroll
2. Make the product feel premium and distinct
3. Make a complex capability understandable in under 2 seconds
4. Create a reusable visual language across LinkedIn, blog, site, and ads
5. Make Celeste look like an operating system for critical environments, not another SaaS tool

If an image does not do at least 2, it failed.

---

## The Aura

The image should communicate one thing fast: **control over complexity under pressure.**

The viewer should feel: "This product is built for serious operations."

---

## The Image Must Feel

- Isolated
- Deliberate
- Premium
- Restrained
- Spacious
- Authoritative
- High-stakes
- Engineered
- Calm under pressure

## The Image Must Never Feel

- Busy
- Generic
- Dashboard-like
- Overly informative
- Glossy SaaS
- Dribbble bait
- Neon sci-fi
- Over-rendered
- Trend-chasing
- Decorative for the sake of it

---

## The Key Creative Rule

**One frame = one idea.**

Do not show five ideas at once.

**Bad:** related entities, badges, lots of rows, multiple status types, too many icons, too much explanatory density.

**Better:** 2-3 linked entities max, one dominant state, one visual tension, one reason to stare for an extra second.

For LinkedIn especially, density kills impact.

---

## 1. Composition

Every composition should feel like the UI is floating in intentional darkness, not pasted on a black background.

**Rules:**
- Strong negative space
- Object is not always centered
- Slightly off-axis framing is often better
- Crop aggressively when helpful
- Card or UI cluster should feel heroized, not merely shown
- Canvas should breathe
- Use asymmetry where it adds authority

**Think:**
- Card low-right with overhead falloff
- Search result emerging from darkness
- One entity stack isolated in space
- Layered depth with soft shadow falloff

**Not:**
- Centered flat screenshot on black

---

## 2. Information Density

Workers must reduce prototype density before rendering imagery.

**For marketing images:**
- 1 main card, not 5 cards
- 2-3 rows, not 5+
- 1 status badge max unless comparison is the point
- 1 primary accent area
- No unnecessary helper text
- Remove anything that does not strengthen the story

You are not building the product here. You are building the image of the product.

---

## 3. Lighting Model

This is where most mockups die.

**Celeste light model:**
- Global environment is ultra-dark
- Faint overhead light from top-left
- Soft falloff across surface
- Subtle rim on upper edges
- Lower edges dimmer and heavier
- Restrained teal energy only where meaning exists
- Glow is atmospheric, never loud
- Shadows must ground the object in space
- Lighting should imply seriousness, not spectacle

**The glow should feel like:** "system intelligence present in the background"

**Not:** "tech startup cyber neon"

---

## 4. Material Feel

UI surfaces should feel almost tactile:

- Smoked glass / dark metal / matte graphite
- Subtle internal gradients
- Crisp but restrained borders
- Clean edge separation
- No muddy blur
- No cheap glow spam
- No overdone glassmorphism

A premium product image usually wins by surface control: edge light, inner shadow, shadow softness, consistent z-depth, disciplined contrast.

---

## 5. Typography in Imagery

Typography should not look like a screenshot accident.

**Rules:**
- Use typography as part of composition
- One clear hierarchy
- IDs in mono are good, but only when they earn their place
- Body copy must stay minimal
- Titles should be readable at thumbnail size
- Labels should not compete with the main line
- The image must still work when viewed tiny in a feed

If text becomes the focal clutter, trim it.

---

## 6. Colour Discipline

The palette is a weapon. Do not improvise.

- Warm neutral edge tint allowed in tiny amounts if it adds material realism
- Whites should be soft, not pure clinical white
- Teal should be meaningful, not sprayed around

**The mistake to avoid:** making the teal the aesthetic instead of the accent.

Teal should act like a quiet signal, not a nightclub.

---

## Hard Rules

1. Do not render full product density for marketing imagery. Reduce to the essential story.
2. Every image must communicate one idea only.
3. Negative space is mandatory, not optional.
4. Screenshots are raw material, not final assets.
5. Build editorial HTML scenes when the prototype is too dense.
6. Use a consistent lighting model with subtle top-left overhead falloff.
7. Teal is an accent of meaning, not a decorative effect.
8. Avoid anything that looks like generic B2B SaaS marketing.
9. Favour restraint over spectacle.
10. The image must still feel powerful when seen as a small LinkedIn thumbnail.

---

## What "Apple-Like" Means for Celeste

**It means:**
- Reduction
- Precision
- Controlled lighting
- Object-first composition
- Premium surfaces
- Emotional restraint
- Confidence through simplicity

**It does not mean:**
- Bright white studio renders
- Soft consumer friendliness
- Playful friendliness
- Lifestyle people photos
- Generic floating devices
- Overclean sterile emptiness

Celeste is darker, more operational, more severe.

This is: **Apple discipline applied to a high-stakes maritime operating system.**

---

## Image Scoring Rubric

Score each image 1-10 on:

1. Scroll stopping power
2. Clarity in under 2 seconds
3. Premium feel
4. Breathing room
5. Brand distinctiveness
6. Cinematic depth
7. Restraint
8. Emotional authority
9. Thumbnail readability
10. Non-genericness

**Any image under 8 in "brand distinctiveness" or "non-genericness" is rejected.**

---

## LinkedIn Post Image Constraints

- Simple
- Bold silhouette
- Minimal text
- Strongest focal contrast
- Only one core message
- Readable at tiny size

## Visual Story Library

Workers must build a reusable library of these scenes:

### Cross-Domain Linking
3 entities only. Work order, equipment, warranty. One clear status signal.

### One Search
Isolated search bar in darkness. Result stack emerging below. Focus ring, depth, authority.

### Evidence Trail
Ledger/history panel. Viewed/edited/signed events. Reduced and severe.

### Fault to Action
Fault card with related next actions. Calm, explicit, controlled.

### Part Certainty
One part surfaced with linked equipment/work order. High clarity, little clutter.

### Under Pressure
Tight crop. One critical status moment. Emotionally heavier lighting.

### System Memory
Document + email + work order thread. Show connection, not volume.

## Worker Roles for Image Production

### Worker 1: Creative Direction Research
Study Apple-like product imagery principles. Extract patterns in composition, lighting, crop, depth, restraint. Map into Celeste's darker brand language. Deliver: visual doctrine, 10 rules, 10 anti-patterns.

### Worker 2: Product Story Selection
Identify which product concepts deserve imagery. Deliver: 10 visual story frames, one core message per frame, what must be visible, what must be removed.

### Worker 3: Scene Composer
Convert prototype into reduced HTML scenes for rendering. Simplify density, change hierarchy, improve whitespace, set camera framing. Deliver: HTML/CSS render scenes.

### Worker 4: Render and Finish
Produce final high-res renders. Test crop variants. Tune light, glow, contrast, shadow. Deliver: final PNG exports in multiple aspect ratios.

### Worker 5: QA / Brand Judge
Reject anything generic, dense, trendy, or standard SaaS. Score against rubric. Approve or reject with brutal notes.

---

## What Workers Are Allowed To Do

- Duplicate existing HTML prototypes
- Strip UI down for marketing compositions
- Build fresh HTML scenes purely for rendered imagery
- Add non-product framing layers (background atmospherics, vignettes, shadow planes, controlled light sources, surface gradients)
- Alter spacing, crop, camera angle, and density
- Create editorial variants of the same UI state for different channels
- Research Apple product imagery, premium editorial interface campaigns, luxury tech composition, and industrial product storytelling
- Translate findings into Celeste's darker, more austere brand system

## What Workers Are Not Allowed To Do

- Randomly invent flashy effects
- Use generic hero gradients
- Use bright cyan glows
- Mimic consumer-tech playfulness
- Cram multiple product messages into one visual
- Default to centered full-frame screenshots
- Overuse blur, glow, or glass
- Create images that feel like Figma dribbble shots
